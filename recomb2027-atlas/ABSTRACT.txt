A shared cell-boundary error can alter several expression counts together in imaging-based spatial transcriptomics. We introduce GeoCert, an exact method for determining which observed cell-by-gene counts remain invariant under a physical boundary-deformation budget and which can change. We show that, for ordered boundaries subject to slope, band, thickness and nuclear constraints, the minimum summed interface displacement for every feasible molecular assignment can be encoded in a single polynomial-size graph with rational capacities. For point transcripts along one interface, we use exact critical heights in a count dynamic program that computes hard-budget optima in polynomial time, even when penalized cuts leave a gap. For disjoint two-label ribbons, an exact coordinate conversion gives physical changed-label area; gluing and min-plus composition then enforce one compatible tissue partition and a shared area budget. We also allow different ribbon widths through a union of complete atlases that preserves every deformation permitted by the primary atlas. On Xenium lung crops and a breast format-test dataset, five atlas families per crop yield 496,604 exact interval rows over 17 budgets. At 4 µm², their union widens 298 lung and 3,595 breast intervals relative to the primary atlas. Across the budget grid, shared geometry tightens independent-molecule outer bounds for 29 of 102 lung and 791 of 2,076 breast cell–gene pairs whose outer interval reaches a width of at least two. The same effect appears in two breast cancer donors: under the primary atlas, shared geometry tightens independent-molecule bounds for 13.8% and 11.7% of eligible cell–gene pairs. For each interval endpoint, we provide a boundary that shows how the reported count is attained and when multiple molecular transfers share one deformation.
